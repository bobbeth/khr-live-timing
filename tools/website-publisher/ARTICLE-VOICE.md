# Mud Mules TV race report voice

The owner prefers punchier race recaps: lively and competitive.

Lead with what matters: the winner, a big climb through the field, a repeat win or an unusually strong result. Use short sentences and familiar racing language. Put drivers and car numbers ahead of statistics. Keep headlines direct and decks useful.

Write about racing, not how the website collected its information. Bridge, SDK, capture, provenance, verified classification and official-backed are internal terms. They do not belong in headlines, reports, labels or promotional copy. Technical fields and validation stay intact in the underlying race data.

Use only supported facts. A start-to-finish gain is not evidence of a particular pass, battle, restart, crash or last-lap move. A win from pole does not mean the driver led every lap. Never invent quotes or write as though the owner personally witnessed an incident without their account.

Avoid stock closing paragraphs, repeated podium recaps, long explanations of missing data and claims about what statistics prove. Leave unavailable figures out. Say a fact once; keep the full session results accessible below the report.

Example: “Eleventh on the grid, first at the flag. Chad Puterbaugh gained ten places to win Bomber Stocks Round 6 at Kern Raceway in the #20.”

Keep factual driver identities in the results. Use owner-approved names or nicknames in prose when available; do not invent nicknames or personal opinions on the owner's behalf.
